# Abstract Geometry

Source: supplied reference board 07, label “Abstract Geometry”. Dimension: graphic shapes and image composition.

## Observed

Bold dark typography, a blue accent, circles and rectangular forms, and an architectural photograph create a composed hero on a light field. The shapes act as graphic partners to the image rather than as data marks. This particular treatment differs from the board's primary-color Bauhaus example without establishing a strict historical taxonomy between them.

## Suggested use and combinations

Consider a small geometric system for a brand introduction, portfolio section, abstract product idea, or editorial opener. Choose shapes whose alignment and scale reinforce the message and give each a clear role. The treatment can combine with a split layout, restrained photography, or strong typography; one dominant composition is usually easier to read than multiple unrelated shape families. Retain existing project colors when appropriate and leave business structure to the responsible page module.

## Limits, assets, and fallback

Shapes should not obscure content, resemble unavailable controls, or imply quantitative relationships without supporting data. Keep important text outside complex intersections and check cropped compositions at practical sizes. Simple SVG or CSS geometry can be inexpensive; photography requires suitable assets, while three-dimensional rendering and animation are optional. If composition becomes fragile, use a single geometric accent and a plain image frame, preserving the original reading order and hierarchy.
