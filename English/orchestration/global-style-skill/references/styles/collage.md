# Collage

Source: supplied board 04, label **Collage**. Dimension: layered image construction and tactile editorial texture. This is a candidate, not a product default.

## Observed evidence

The example combines a cream background with photographic cutouts, torn-paper edges, tape-like details, handwriting, and overlapping layers. Its palette and composition are calmer than the neighboring Maximalism sample. The image does not demonstrate movable pieces, physical simulation, or interactive handwriting.

## Suggested adoption

Consider this for personal stories, travel, culture, creative portfolios, and selected editorial sections. Establish a reading sequence first, then use layers to connect or emphasize related material. Distinguish meaningful captions from decorative scribbles. Keep headings and essential copy as readable live text rather than embedding them inside a flattened collage.

## Combinations and constraints

Combine with `editorial` for narrative, `hand-drawn` for limited annotation, or `minimalism` for open space around the composition. Collage does not require a dense page. Avoid covering actionable regions, scattering related text across distant fragments, or using torn edges that look like clipped content. Ordinary business controls should retain clear boundaries.

## Assets, cost, and fallback

Cutout preparation, consistent textures, and responsive repositioning add asset work. A static composition is often adequate. When layers cannot remain legible on small screens, present the same images and captions in a straightforward ordered sequence with a few retained tactile accents.
