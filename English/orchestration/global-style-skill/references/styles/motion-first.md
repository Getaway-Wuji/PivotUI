# Motion-first

Source: supplied board 04, label **Motion-first**. Dimension: motion-oriented composition and a potential experience direction. This is a candidate, not a product default.

## Observed evidence

The still example contains overlapping tilted panels, repeated diagonal lettering, and a purple-black composition that suggests movement. It cannot establish actual animation, timing, scrolling behavior, transitions, or responsiveness. Treat the visible composition and the board's motion label as different kinds of evidence.

## Suggested adoption

Consider motion-led work for a public brand story or expressive portfolio when movement serves a concrete narrative. Supplement the reference with a purpose for each animation, its trigger, stable reading state, interruption behavior, and reduced-motion alternative. Do not invent a scroll sequence and describe it as observed.

## Combinations and constraints

Combine with `minimalism` to leave attention for movement or `collage` for a controlled layered composition. Keep actions and reading order dependable without motion. Do not transfer immersive marketing sequences to dashboards or repetitive business tasks; those need task-related feedback. Avoid forced scrolling, delayed access to content, and motion that continually competes with text.

## Assets, cost, and fallback

Animation requires separate implementation and testing across input methods and devices. Begin with a complete static layout. Preserve that layout for reduced-motion preferences, limited performance, or unavailable animation support. A style choice alone does not select an animation library or external skill.
