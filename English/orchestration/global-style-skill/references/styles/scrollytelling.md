# Scrollytelling

Source: supplied reference board 08, label “Scrollytelling”. Dimension: narrative organization and suggested interaction pattern.

## Observed

The example shows a large landscape hero, a traveller, an editorial headline, numbered chapter navigation, and a scroll cue. These cues suggest a sequential story. The static screenshot does not prove parallax, pinned scenes, scroll-linked animation, or any particular implementation library.

## Suggested use and combinations

Consider chapter-based presentation when supplied content has a meaningful narrative progression, such as a journey, explanation, or public product story. Decide the reading sequence and chapter purpose before adding effects. Photographic, editorial, or other visual directions can supply the appearance; scrollytelling itself is not a palette. Give the page owner responsibility for content and the interaction owner responsibility for any requested behavior. Selecting this reference alone does not authorize another skill's full workflow.

## Limits, assets, and fallback

Keep navigation, key actions, and an understandable reading path available. Do not make essential information depend on precise scrolling or motion. If animation is chosen, provide keyboard access and reduced-motion behavior appropriate to the implementation. Static chapters may be sufficient; animated scenes require additional assets and verification. For operational tasks, use only a justified local explanation rather than reorganizing the whole application as a story. Fall back to ordinary document scrolling with clearly separated chapters and visible navigation.
