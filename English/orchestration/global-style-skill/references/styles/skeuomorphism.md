# Skeuomorphism

Source: supplied board 02, label **Skeuomorphism**. Dimension: material imagery and possible physical metaphor. This is a candidate, not a product default.

## Observed evidence

The example presents tangible objects such as a camera, books, wood, and stationery with warm photographic textures. This supports material richness and a physical-world mood. It does not prove that controls imitate real objects or behave like mechanical devices; the board's label is broader than the observable interface evidence.

## Suggested adoption

Consider the observed treatment for craft, lifestyle, object collections, and product storytelling. Borrow material texture, lighting, and considered object placement. If a physical control metaphor is requested, separately specify its purpose, recognizable affordance, keyboard operation, and ordinary alternative instead of inferring these from the photograph.

## Combinations and constraints

Combine with `editorial` for narrative or `minimalism` to keep imagery dominant. Do not turn decorative objects into unlabeled actions. In business tools, reserve rich materials for relevant content or a limited accent; repetitive controls must stay easy to scan and interpret.

## Assets, cost, and fallback

Convincing imagery and consistent lighting carry most of the cost; interactive simulations add separate implementation work. When suitable assets are unavailable, use an existing product photograph and plain controls. Keep texture out of small text backgrounds and data comparison areas.
