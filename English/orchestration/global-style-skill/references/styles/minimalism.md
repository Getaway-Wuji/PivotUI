# Minimalism

Source: supplied board 01, label **Minimalism**. Dimension: visual hierarchy and restraint. This is a candidate, not a product default.

## Observed evidence

The example uses a quiet neutral background, a large dark heading, sparse navigation, a single prominent action, substantial whitespace, and one product image. It supports an uncluttered visual direction; the screenshot does not establish exact fonts, spacing values, or behavior.

## Suggested adoption

Consider this for a focused product introduction, portfolio, or calm application surface. Rank content before removing it. Establish a few purposeful type levels, shared alignment lines, restrained accents, and clear action priority. For business tools, retain labels, task context, useful density, and visible controls instead of copying the hero's large empty areas.

## Combinations and constraints

Combine with `swiss` for alignment, `editorial` for reading rhythm, or `bento-grid` for grouped content. Do not equate simplicity with small pale text, hidden navigation, or missing feedback. Test long copy and mixed-language headings rather than assuming the example's short text.

## Assets, cost, and fallback

Layout cost is generally modest; quality depends on typography and relevant imagery. If the hero asset is unavailable, use a text-led composition or an existing product image, keeping hierarchy intact. Additional interaction and narrow-screen choices are supplemental decisions.
