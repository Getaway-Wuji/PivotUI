# Maximalism

Source: supplied board 04, label **Maximalism**. Dimension: expressive visual abundance and layered composition. This is a candidate, not a product default.

## Observed evidence

The example combines saturated colors, varied textures, stickers, sculpture and photographic fragments, and large display lettering. Many elements occupy the scene, but the main heading remains a focal point. A dense screenshot does not establish useful information density or interactive layers.

## Suggested adoption

Consider this for cultural campaigns, music, creative collectives, and expressive brand sections where abundance supports a clear message. Choose a dominant focal point, a coherent image family, and a deliberate hierarchy of secondary elements. Keep decorative density separate from the amount of information users must process.

## Combinations and constraints

Combine with `collage` for image construction or `editorial` for disciplined content sequencing. Reserve quiet areas for actions and essential copy. Avoid extending every decorative device to forms, tables, and error messages. On narrow screens, reduce or reposition ornamental layers while retaining meaningful content and its order. Overlap must not obstruct focus or touch targets.

## Assets, cost, and fallback

Multiple coordinated assets, compositing, and responsive art direction create substantial work. Use available relevant imagery rather than padding the page with unrelated objects. If assets or screen space are limited, reduce the composition to a few strong layers and preserve the same focal hierarchy.
