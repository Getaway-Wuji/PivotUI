# Visual Recipes

P01–P11 consolidate methods across sources. The six data styles in the images and eight video compositions are not mutually exclusive: a dense card wall is a P04 variant, and magazine composition is a P01 variant. Choose the [A–G task structure](selection-guide.md) before the main recipe. Industry examples guide suitability; they are not mandatory pairings.

## Apply recipes consistently

Extract each recipe's **visual focus, surface relationships, layout rhythm, and data representation**, then map them to existing project variables. Borrow useful organization methods from other references, state their roles, and unify fonts, borders, radii, and series colors. Do not impose a fixed limit on the number of references.

White, gray, blue, and green here describe visual observations rather than measurements. Only the P09 warm-peach variant includes exact parameters from a source prompt. Without brand standards, propose implementation values explicitly as recommendations for this task; do not backfill them as an established project style.

## Quick reference

Recommended structures are starting points. Existing brands and explicit requirements take precedence. Adopt useful hierarchy and organization, then adapt colors and materials. For example, a dark-brand business overview can combine the P04 grid and P05 surfaces without becoming behavioral analysis merely to fit a category.

## P01 Editorial narrative

**Sources:** S26; V06 is a magazine-composition variant. See the [source summary](reference-index.md) for IDs; the necessary methods are distilled below.

- **Structure:** business conclusion/question title → a few evidence metrics → wide main trend chart → explanatory charts/text → next step. A large title, main chart, and one important finding establish reading order.
- **Surfaces:** white or very pale cool background, dark body text, limited blue emphasis; fine rules can separate sections without rounding every section into a card. V06 can contribute a pale-blue page and generous title treatment without requiring its AI chat panel.
- **Text:** titles state a specific business question or supported conclusion. Use neutral topics until data supports a finding. Wrapped Chinese titles still align with the main chart edge.
- **Charts:** clearly distinguish current/comparison periods and source important events. Secondary charts answer questions about loss, composition, or groups.
- **Fits:** periodic reviews, business explanations, growth reports. Analysis pages may use it while retaining filters.
- **Avoid:** long copy obscuring evidence, oversized titles for magazine styling, unsupported AI insights for every KPI. Urgent live-response contexts should put alerts first.

## P02 Monochrome minimalism

**Sources:** S27; financial organization from S17/S18 may be borrowed separately.

- **Structure:** enlarge one number only when it truly has top priority. Scale other metrics by hierarchy; align trends, target comparisons, rankings, and tables precisely. Do not force a giant number when no metric is uniquely primary.
- **Surfaces:** white background, black text, pale-gray separators, one accent for the current object or emphasis. Limit shadows and nested cards.
- **Text:** bind numbers to units and periods; use consistent precision, alignment, and numeral widths in comparisons. Weight differences establish hierarchy.
- **Fits:** executive reports, precise reading, financial overviews, publication-like business data pages.
- **Avoid:** deleting definitions for whitespace or excessively faint axis labels. Add line styles/direct labels when multiple series cannot be distinguished, and data colors when needed.

## P03 Soft light surfaces

**Sources:** S28; business examples S14–S16 and S19.

- **Structure:** a clear but light grid; give main trends or tasks more area than explanatory cards. Retain real people, calendar, or task objects where relevant.
- **Surfaces:** near-white/very pale gray backgrounds; muted blue-teal or lavender support colors; light boundaries and moderate radii. Keep broad surfaces quiet while text and important lines remain distinct.
- **Charts:** give current series clearer color and recede historical series. Include retention heatmaps, frequency bars, or hierarchical charts only when data supports them.
- **Fits:** daily user operations, health records, team/people management, light products used for long periods.
- **Avoid:** pastel pills for every label, severe risks as visually weak as ordinary states, or similar series colors with no other distinction.

## P04 Modular grid

**Sources:** S29, S16, S17; V04's dense card wall is one variant.

- **Structure:** allocate the largest area to the main trend/task; size metrics, rankings, progress, and summaries by importance. Options include a top metric strip over main/secondary areas, or a narrow metric column beside a wide chart and information sidebar.
- **Surfaces:** white modules on a light background, limited cobalt or project accent color; alignment and area scale establish order. Shared edges and spacing matter more than card count.
- **V04 variant:** top navigation, first-row metrics, a wide chart beneath beside a narrow status panel, consistent card boundaries. Shared radii unify the reference, but its sample radius is not universal.
- **Fits:** multi-topic business overviews, operational workspaces, views comparing multiple dimensions simultaneously.
- **Avoid:** equal-sized cards everywhere, large blank areas under short lists to match bottom edges, or tiny text to fit everything on one screen. Increase density by removing repetition and containers before shrinking numbers.

## P05 Dark graphite console

**Sources:** S30 shows product-behavior analysis; V08 shows growth analysis. Monitoring is an extension proposed by this skill.

- **Structure:** preserve the actual analysis/monitoring task. Dark surfaces neither change the task automatically nor require server logs.
- **Surfaces:** use a few clear levels for background, cards, and overlays. Distinguish primary/secondary text and borders. Teal-green and orange in different sources are alternatives, not colors to copy together.
- **Charts:** clearly contrast the primary series; recede secondary grids and series. Use selection and explanation to simplify complex paths. Avoid glowing every line.
- **Fits:** a requested dark theme, an existing dark product system, specialist analysis, or low-light work. Do not select it merely because the request says “premium.”
- **Avoid:** indistinguishable dark surfaces, unlabeled thin red/green lines, and several equally dominant black blocks. V08's brightness percentages are commentary, not a color standard.

## P06 Muted natural palette

**Sources:** S31; S17/S18 provide another business example in green finance.

- **Structure:** relate target, actual, baseline, and change over time through fine but clear trend lines, comparison bars, progress, and resource details.
- **Surfaces:** white/pale gray-green, deep teal/sage, limited warm yellow. Emphasis comes from data relationships rather than leaf illustrations.
- **Fits:** environment, energy, resource efficiency, sustainability reports, and appropriately branded personal resource overviews.
- **Explanation:** total consumption/emissions and efficiency differ. When comparable output data exists, show both total and intensity per unit of output to separate scale changes from efficiency changes. Without the denominator, do not infer intensity or claim improved efficiency.
- **Avoid:** coloring every increase green. Higher emissions/consumption may be unfavorable; use metric definitions. Make cross-year definition changes visible; smoothing must not change the meaning of raw trends.

## P07 Large color-block sections

**Sources:** S05, V01; related store-operations examples.

- **Structure:** group a few major business topics, such as throughput, capacity, location, and timing. Keep numbers, charts, and actions together within each section. Color aids recognition; headings explain meaning.
- **Surfaces:** coral, purple, pale cyan, or gray-green section backgrounds within a dark-neutral shell; actual colors still follow brand and semantics.
- **Fits:** a few parallel business areas, quick scanning, multi-site operations, team execution panels.
- **Avoid:** a dozen strong card colors, confusing section backgrounds with chart encodings, or a red topic section that appears to signal an alarm throughout. Do not copy the adjacent AI panel by default.

## P08 Image-card feed

**Sources:** V02, S37.

- **Structure:** search/categories plus a main content-card area, with a sidebar or selected details when useful. Match image crop ratios to object recognition; put names, times, and states in stable positions.
- **Surfaces:** neutral light backgrounds and light boundaries let images supply color. Reduce image prominence when content is insufficient; do not fill gaps with unrelated stock images.
- **Fits:** portfolios, trips, courses, assets, and media discovery. A Dashboard that serves as a homepage does not need forced trend charts.
- **Avoid:** replacing essential business/financial areas with landscape cards, interrupting comparisons with differently sized decorative images, or removing usable access to objects without images.

## P09 Glassmorphism

**Sources:** V03 cool-blue glass; S20–S25 warm-peach glass; S14 also has a light glass treatment.

- **Structure:** complete the chosen task layout first. Gradient backgrounds and translucent surfaces are material treatments. Choose cool blue or warm peach rather than stacking both background systems.
- **Surfaces:** keep backgrounds soft and low-frequency; limit main-panel and nested-module layers. Dense text uses solid or nearly solid surfaces; a local focal point can use a dark card.
- **Fits:** low-to-medium density personal/team workspaces, cloud-resource presentation, and explicitly requested branded overviews.
- **Avoid:** stacked blur layers, data text over complex backgrounds, heavy shadows concealing edges, or light tracking, flipping, and bouncing on every card.

### Warm-peach source parameters

These exact S25 prompt parameters are optional starting points for this recipe, not established target-project tokens or CSS measured from a screenshot:

| Purpose | Original prompt parameter | Application |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Background | `#E4C0A6` / `#D4B8AC` / `#EBD7CC` | Soft gradient; keep chart backgrounds quiet |
| Glass | `blur(28px) saturate(140%)`; white at 45%–60% “transparency” | The source wording is ambiguous; define an rgba alpha. If using 0.55, record it as this implementation's choice and check readability |
| Boundary | 1px translucent white border, top inner highlight | Check boundaries against both light and dark backgrounds |
| Shape | 24px radius, broad soft shadow | Use only when no existing standard applies and this variant is selected |
| Stable surface | `#F3F1F1` | Suitable for rankings/body text |
| Dark card | `#1B1B19` | Reserve for a clear focal point; not mandatory on every page |
| Accent | `#B66D44` | Define brand selection and status semantics separately |
| Chart | Vertical gradient `#E7A471` → `#C5A0AB` | Must not encode an undefined additional variable |
| Typography | Plus Jakarta Sans / Manrope; headings 48px or larger | Source suggestion only; add Chinese fallbacks and reduce titles for dense/narrow layouts |

Charts and lists can continue the source's light treatment: thin round-ended bars for a small set of categories, with true baselines and values; short lists separated through whitespace, alignment, and text hierarchy instead of heavy rules. Retain necessary boundaries for dense content or precise row comparison; material styling must not weaken navigation within data.

Without blur support, or when reduced transparency is needed, fall back to solid surfaces in the same palette, borders, and light shadows. Product usability must not depend on glass effects.

## P10 Neon accents

**Sources:** V05; S06 also uses bright green for actions/progress in a workspace.

- **Structure:** one clear main content axis; repeated functional cards share a structure. Concentrate bright color on the current focus, main metric, or main action in one or a few places.
- **Surfaces:** near-white, black/gray, or the existing theme as base, with limited yellow-green. Keep labels readable on bright colors. Do not fill space by copying the source's instruction to duplicate a card four times.
- **Fits:** workflows, automation, development records, quotas, and team tools when the brand supports it.
- **Avoid:** highlighting every KPI, status label, chart line, and button simultaneously; neon competing with severe alerts.

## P11 Dark header

**Source:** V07, a health-overview example.

- **Structure:** one dark header carries identity/topic and limited navigation; light content carries actual data. A title may use one serif/italic expression, while numeric comparisons remain consistent.
- **Surfaces:** dark brown-black, warm white, and limited amber, or the brand's corresponding light/dark pairing.
- **Fits:** personal overviews, membership/health records, brand homepages with low urgency and a welcoming context.
- **Avoid:** a first-screen-filling header without useful information, equally heavy black cards beneath it, or copying health thresholds/advice. Size the header for important-content visibility on actual screens instead of preserving the video's area ratio.

## Implementation decisions across recipes

1. What should appear first: the main metric, chart, exception, or task? Concentrate area, position, and contrast on it.
2. Which relationships stay stable: units, time, series colors, shared controls, and surface hierarchy? Recipes change appearance, not data meaning.
3. What should be removed: repeated explanations, unnecessary containers, decorative images, unsupported AI insights, and filler sidebars? Preserve information needed to complete the task.
4. Does real content work: Chinese headings, long names, large/negative numbers, empty data, extreme values, more rows, and narrow screens? A polished screenshot without these adaptations is not a completed design.
