# Split Screen

Source: supplied reference board 08, label “Split Screen”. Dimension: layout composition.

## Observed

The hero uses a broad vertical division: a pale text area on one side and blue-toned architectural photography on the other, with simple navigation above. Each side has a distinct visual role. The screenshot shows one wide-screen arrangement; it does not establish fixed equal widths, viewport-height requirements, or a narrow-screen solution.

## Suggested use and combinations

Consider a split when two content roles benefit from direct adjacency, such as explanation and evidence, text and product image, or two genuinely comparable choices. Let content importance determine the proportions. This layout can combine with monochrome photography, restrained brand direction, geometric decoration, or other treatments without becoming a separate palette or component system. The page owner decides structure and reading order; the responsive owner adapts the arrangement to available space.

## Limits, assets, and fallback

Avoid forcing unrelated content into a symmetrical pair or shrinking text to preserve the division. On narrow screens, stack or otherwise reorganize the regions while keeping the main message and action in a sensible sequence. Photography requires an appropriate crop, but a split can also use useful text or a product preview. Layout itself is inexpensive; fragile fixed heights add unnecessary work. Fall back to unequal columns or a clear single-column sequence when content or space demands it.
