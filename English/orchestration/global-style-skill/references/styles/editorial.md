# Editorial

Source: supplied board 01, label **Editorial**. Dimension: reading hierarchy and image-led composition. This is a candidate, not a product default.

## Observed evidence

The example uses prominent serif and italic display text, a quiet paper-like background, a large photograph, and smaller story previews. It resembles a publication landing page. The screenshot establishes visual arrangement, not a complete article layout or reading interaction.

## Suggested adoption

Consider this for journals, cultural sites, case studies, brand stories, and portfolios with substantial narrative. Establish a clear relationship among headline, summary, image, caption, and related material. Give reading copy a comfortable measure and separate display typography from everyday controls. Use real content length when determining hierarchy.

## Combinations and constraints

Combine with `swiss` for a disciplined grid, `minimalism` for quieter presentation, or `collage` for selected story illustrations. For a business application, limit editorial expression to appropriate summaries or reports; keep tabular data and recurring controls efficient. Decorative italics and display serifs should not weaken small labels, numerals, or mixed-language readability.

## Assets, cost, and fallback

Photography selection, cropping, and coordinated fonts require more attention than basic layout. If imagery is limited, use a text-led opening and purposeful captions. Missing images should not erase the reading order or create unexplained empty columns. Responsive story sequencing is a supplemental decision.
