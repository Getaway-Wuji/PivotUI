## Useful visual relationships

- A multi-color gradient is concentrated in the upper marketing region, while detailed reading returns to white or near-white. Treat the visual change as a content transition, not a background for every panel.
- The source lists indigo action `#533afd`, navy ink `#0d253d`, white canvas and soft canvas `#f6f9fc`. Decorative ruby/magenta gradient colors have a different job from the primary action.
- It lists 56px display text at weight 300 versus 16px body, and tabular-number settings for financial comparisons. Rounded primary buttons use 8px 16px padding; feature/pricing cards use 32px padding. These values describe the source candidate, not minimum usable sizes.

## Transfer into the target product

For a financial product marketing page, confine atmosphere to the introductory region and let pricing or feature detail use calmer reading surfaces. Map primary-action, text and background roles to the existing product theme; choose a gradient only when appropriate to that region and available implementation budget.

For a financial table, tabular numerals can be a useful typographic candidate, but preserve units, precision, alignment and business statuses from the product. A marketing gradient or navy mockup does not establish a dark dashboard theme. Do not copy the 300 body weight without testing legibility in the actual font and language.

## Evidence limits and completion needs

The source's dashboard composites are static, and registration is not a complete account-management workflow. Its exact font, breakpoint and touch/compliance assertions were not independently checked. Proprietary Sohne is not supplied. Keep decorative and chart/status colors separate; a visual example cannot rename a business status.

When implementing, complete keyboard, focus, loading, error and narrow-screen behavior for the actual content, and check real contrast. Case values are not library-wide Token defaults or an accessibility pass.
