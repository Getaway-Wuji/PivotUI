# Isometric

Source: supplied reference board 06, label “Isometric”. Dimension: illustrative perspective and spatial explanation.

## Observed

The hero places text beside a blue and pale three-dimensional arrangement of buildings, platforms, small people, and trees. Repeated viewing angles make separate elements read as one constructed scene. The image shows a visual metaphor; it does not establish the product's actual architecture, workflow, or interactive model.

## Suggested use and combinations

Consider a coherent isometric scene when explaining a system, service relationship, environment, or product concept that benefits from spatial grouping. Begin with the actual explanation and decide which elements the illustration needs. Pair the scene with simple typography and a clear text sequence. It can combine with restrained brand colors or modular content sections, provided perspective and asset treatment remain consistent. The page owner retains the explanatory order; perspective should not dictate business relationships.

## Limits, assets, and fallback

Small details and overlapping objects can become illegible on narrow screens. Essential labels and relationships need readable equivalents outside a decorative miniature scene. Do not imply integrations or capabilities merely because an object appears connected. Custom illustration can be costly, while one supplied static asset may be sufficient; live three-dimensional rendering is optional. If assets or space are limited, fall back to a simpler two-dimensional flow or grouped block diagram grounded in the same explanation.
