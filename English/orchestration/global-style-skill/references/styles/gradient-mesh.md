# Gradient Mesh

Source: supplied reference board 06, label “Gradient Mesh”. Dimension: background color and lighting treatment.

## Observed

Broad, diffuse pink, blue, and purple transitions fill the hero behind a large headline and a translucent-looking feature panel. The color field has several soft centers rather than one sharply defined stripe. The static image does not establish animation, a shader, true transparency, or reliable contrast across every part of the composition.

## Suggested use and combinations

Consider a bounded mesh-like background for an introductory or promotional region when atmosphere supports the message. Use the target product's adopted color roles rather than importing a new palette automatically. Pair the diffuse field with clear typography and stable surfaces for important content. It can accompany a simple split layout or a product preview; avoid adding competing textures and multiple strong lighting effects without a specific reason.

## Limits, assets, and fallback

Evaluate actual text, icons, borders, and controls against the changing background beneath them. A solid content surface may be more readable than a glass effect. Do not rely on the gradient to communicate status or data categories. A static image or modest CSS layering can deliver the treatment; continuous animation and canvas effects add work not required by this reference. Fall back to a subtle simple gradient or solid surface when contrast, performance, or asset constraints make the richer field unsuitable.
