# Neo-Brutalism

Source: supplied board 02, label **Neo-Brutalism**. Dimension: bold component language and playful graphic contrast. This is a candidate, not a product default.

## Observed evidence

The example uses bright flat color blocks, thick dark outlines, hard offset shadows, bold text, and overlapping window-like illustrations. It is more colorful and explicitly framed than the neighboring Brutalism example. Visible overlaps do not establish drag behavior or interactive windows.

## Suggested adoption

Consider this for playful creative products, campaign pages, and selected expressive features. Use a consistent outline and shadow system, with one primary action clearly prioritized. Keep decorative windows separate from actual dialogs and controls. Reserve stronger colors for meaningful hierarchy rather than giving every region equal emphasis.

## Combinations and constraints

Combine with `bento-grid` for modular grouping or `hand-drawn` for limited illustration accents. Separate brand decoration from error, warning, and success meanings. Dense business pages may retain a small amount of this language through headers or accents while reducing repeated heavy frames. Verify text readability inside saturated blocks and keep focus distinguishable from decorative borders.

## Assets, cost, and fallback

Flat shapes and CSS shadows can keep asset costs modest; bespoke illustrations add work. When layouts become crowded, reduce overlaps and use fewer accent surfaces. A simpler outlined treatment should retain the same controls, reading order, and content hierarchy.
