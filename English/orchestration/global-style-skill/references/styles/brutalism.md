# Brutalism

Source: supplied board 02, label **Brutalism**. Dimension: raw graphic hierarchy and direct presentation. This is a candidate, not a product default.

## Observed evidence

The example combines oversized heavy typography, a stark monochrome palette, thin rules, a raw concrete photograph, and a plain rectangular action. Its expressive quality comes from scale and material contrast. The screenshot does not show intentionally broken navigation or unconventional interaction.

## Suggested adoption

Consider this for cultural projects, expressive portfolios, or brand pages that benefit from forceful directness. Choose a dominant typographic statement and a clear content sequence. Let visible structure and honest imagery carry the expression. Keep utility controls recognizable even when display type is emphatic.

## Combinations and constraints

Combine with `swiss` for alignment or `editorial` for a clear reading sequence. `neo-brutalism` adds a different colorful, outlined component language; adopt that deliberately rather than treating the names as interchangeable. Avoid clipping meaningful headings, unreadable all-caps paragraphs, arbitrary overlaps, or disruptive scrolling. A raw appearance does not require poor access to content or actions.

## Assets, cost, and fallback

Basic implementation can remain simple; distinctive typography and photography need careful selection. If the dramatic asset or font is unavailable, retain the strong hierarchy, rules, and restrained palette with project resources. Adapt oversized headings to narrow screens instead of shrinking all content indiscriminately.
