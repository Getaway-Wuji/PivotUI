# Conditional patterns across cases

Use when comparing a reusable relationship. These are authored adaptations from the linked third-party analyses, not measured universal laws. Read the selected case for its scope and evidence; a pattern does not require loading every linked card.

## P01 — Separate surfaces before adding decoration

[Linear](cases/linear.app.md) uses a dark surface ladder and fine borders; [IBM](cases/ibm.md) describes white/gray bands and square tiles; [Vercel](cases/vercel.md) describes near-white surfaces and neutral boundaries. Their common mechanism is legible grouping with a small number of purposeful surface roles, although their actual values and shapes differ.

Use when panels need grouping without each requiring a strong shadow. Choose the minimum levels the target content needs; map page, panel and boundary roles to the product theme. For dense applications, verify selected, focus and disabled states remain legible. Do not copy an entire surface ladder or replace the existing radius system because one example uses it.

## P02 — Give actions and decoration different jobs

[Stripe](cases/stripe.md) places atmosphere in marketing gradients while reserving a consistent CTA treatment; [Notion](cases/notion.md) distinguishes its primary-action and ordinary-link roles; [Kraken](cases/kraken.md) separates purple emphasis from success badges.

Use when an interface's colors compete for attention. Identify the actual action hierarchy, then assign brand, secondary-link, decorative and semantic roles deliberately. Existing business status/chart meanings prevail. A single marketing CTA color does not imply one permitted color for the entire application, and gradients need not enter its data regions.

## P03 — Match visual weight to the user's next decision

[Linear](cases/linear.app.md) lets product images explain software; [Airbnb](cases/airbnb.md) places imagery near comparable property information and a booking action; [Stripe](cases/stripe.md) separates promotional atmosphere from more detailed reading areas.

Use when imagery has an actual explanatory or comparison job. Decide what readers must learn from it, retain adjacent labels needed to interpret it, and establish a useful fallback when assets are unavailable. Marketing screenshots remain evidence about presentation, not proof of the product workflow they depict. Long Chinese copy and small screens may change the image/text balance.

## P04 — Use type roles before multiplying sizes

[SAP](cases/sap.md) describes equal-sized navigation and list text with different weights; [IBM](cases/ibm.md) contrasts light display text with stronger emphasis; [Vercel](cases/vercel.md) distinguishes prose from technical labels using sans and mono roles.

Use when multiple text classes compete within a narrow range. Start with semantic roles and content, then use available weights, spacing and alignment where they are distinguishable. Choose a separate size only when it improves hierarchy. Re-test substitutions and multilingual content; proprietary fonts, extreme tracking and subtle weight differences may not transfer. Numeric alignment for comparison is separate from decorative use of mono.

These patterns supplement existing design methods. Product-specific adoption belongs in the target project's records and single Token source; adding a case does not amend the library's shared foundations.
