# Monoline Illustration

Source: supplied reference board 06, label “Monoline Illustration”. Dimension: illustration and line language.

## Observed

The example combines friendly line drawings of a person, plants, and everyday objects with a pale field, limited warm accents, and hand-drawn-looking display lettering. Similar stroke treatment makes the objects read as a family. The screenshot does not establish a complete icon set, font family, or scalable illustration specification.

## Suggested use and combinations

Consider this asset language for an approachable explanation, educational section, lifestyle introduction, or onboarding illustration when its subject supports the content. Keep stroke character and detail level coherent across related assets. Pair line drawings with ordinary readable body typography and clear controls; handwriting is an optional display accent, not an instruction to restyle all interface text. A restrained layout or simple color field can provide enough support without additional decorative systems.

## Limits, assets, and fallback

Fine lines and small internal details may disappear at reduced sizes or against textured backgrounds. Preserve the meaning of explanatory imagery in readable text, and let purely decorative drawings remain nonessential. Asset selection or custom illustration is the main cost; animation is not required. If a consistent set is unavailable, use one suitable illustration or a simpler line symbol with clear text. Do not mix unrelated stroke styles merely to populate every section.
