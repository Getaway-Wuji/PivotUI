# Monochrome Photography

Source: supplied reference board 07, label “Monochrome Photography”. Dimension: photography treatment and editorial composition.

## Observed

The example emphasizes grayscale photographic subjects, a large dark serif headline, sparse navigation, and open space. Contrast between image, type, and background organizes attention without a broad decorative palette. The screenshot demonstrates one editorial arrangement; it does not require every product photograph or functional color to become monochrome.

## Suggested use and combinations

Consider this direction when portraiture, place, product form, or a visual story deserves emphasis and suitable imagery is available. Pair a deliberate crop with a clear headline and restrained supporting copy. It can combine with a split layout or a quiet brand presentation, while an established accent color can remain reserved for meaningful actions. Let the page owner preserve the story order and practical content requirements; image treatment alone does not decide them.

## Limits, assets, and fallback

Check that grayscale conversion does not remove information needed to understand a product or compare options. Functional status distinctions still require clear labels and the product's established encoding. Large images need narrow-screen crops that retain their meaningful subject; overlaid text needs reliable contrast. Appropriate supplied photography is the main dependency. If the image cannot support a dominant role, use a smaller monochrome photograph within a readable typographic layout, or keep necessary product images in color.
