## Useful visual relationships

- Builds nearly all chrome from quiet light (`#ffffff`, `#fafafa`, hairlines `#dfdfdf` / `#ededed`) and dark (`#1c1c1c`, `#202020`) neutrals with charcoal ink (`#171717`, secondary `#707070`), so one emerald green accent (`#3ecf8e`, pressed `#24b47e`) stands out immediately as the primary action cue.
- Pairs dark ink (`#171717`)—not white text—on the emerald primary button (`#3ecf8e`) to preserve legible button contrast.
- Uses tight, restrained radii (`4px–8px` on controls and cards) and compact `8px 16px` button padding suited to technical consoles and dense product layouts.

## Transfer into the target product

For developer consoles, cloud control planes, and B2B data tools, borrow the chromatic restraint: keep surfaces, borders, and secondary controls monochrome so primary actions and genuine status indicators never fight decorative color noise. Map the single accent role to the target product's own brand token and always check foreground/background contrast before choosing dark vs white text on mid-luminance brand buttons.

## Evidence limits and completion needs
