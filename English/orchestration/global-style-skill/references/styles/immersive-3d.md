# 3D / Immersive

Source: supplied board 03, label **3D / Immersive**. Dimension: spatial imagery and scene-led presentation. This is a candidate, not a product default.

## Observed evidence

The example places a rendered floating island and building beside simple copy and an action. Atmospheric depth and scale make the scene dominant. This is evidence for a 3D-looking visual, not proof of real-time rendering, camera movement, object manipulation, or immersion in use.

## Suggested adoption

Consider this for product storytelling, entertainment, architecture, or a focused showcase where spatial imagery explains the subject. First decide whether a still render already meets the need. Specify interactive 3D only when the requested task benefits from exploration or manipulation, with an explicit owner for behavior and implementation.

## Combinations and constraints

Combine with `minimalism` for clear copy or `editorial` for a narrated sequence. Keep primary content and actions available independently of the scene. Business workflows should not require navigating a decorative 3D environment. Preserve reading order and avoid cropping away the subject on narrow screens.

## Assets, cost, and fallback

Model creation and rendering require assets; real-time 3D adds runtime, loading, input, and device constraints. These are different budgets. Provide a static poster or simpler product image when real-time delivery is unsuitable, keeping essential information and actions usable. Motion remains a separate decision.
