# Glassmorphism

Source: supplied board 01, label **Glassmorphism**. Dimension: translucent surfaces and atmospheric layering. This is a candidate, not a product default.

## Observed evidence

The example places softly outlined translucent panels over a blue-violet landscape and gradient background. Rounded boundaries, pale text, and blurred-looking layers create depth. The image cannot confirm actual backdrop-filter behavior, transparency values, or moving backgrounds.

## Suggested adoption

Consider this for selected marketing surfaces, a product hero, or a small atmospheric overlay. Establish a stable backdrop and distinguish foreground content from decorative scenery. Use transparency sparingly where content remains readable across the full background, not only one favorable crop.

## Combinations and constraints

Combine with `minimalism` for clear hierarchy or `dark-tech` for restrained atmospheric presentation. Avoid stacking several translucent layers or spreading blur across dense tables and forms. Text, input boundaries, focus, errors, and selected states need reliable contrast; a surface's visual softness does not define its interaction state. Check actual rendered combinations before adopting values.

## Assets, cost, and fallback

Cost depends on background assets and the number and size of filtered regions. A static image does not require animated scenery. Provide an opaque or lightly tinted surface with visible borders when filtering is unavailable, costly, or reduces readability. Keep all content and actions usable in that fallback.
