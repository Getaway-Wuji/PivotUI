# Swiss

Source: supplied board 01, label **Swiss**. Dimension: typographic organization and grid discipline. This is a candidate, not a product default.

## Observed evidence

The example combines strong column alignment, thin dividing rules, oversized bold type, a limited high-contrast palette, and a distinct accent panel. Its hierarchy comes largely from scale and placement rather than decoration. No particular font family or grid measurements can be confirmed.

## Suggested adoption

Consider this for structured company pages, information-led portfolios, or clearly organized business interfaces. Define primary alignment lines and a readable hierarchy before adding color. Use accent regions to signal content priority, not merely to fill an empty column. Adapt headline scale to the actual content and task.

## Combinations and constraints

Combine with `minimalism` for restraint, `editorial` for long-form content, or `bento-grid` when separate content groups need different footprints. A desktop grid must have a meaningful reading order when stacked. Avoid forcing narrow uppercase lettering onto long labels, translated copy, form fields, or dense data. Keep rules distinct enough to clarify grouping without enclosing every item.

## Assets, cost, and fallback

Asset requirements are low; font availability and careful alignment dominate the work. Use compatible installed or project fonts if the display face is unavailable. Preserve hierarchy through size, weight, and spacing instead of simulated condensed lettering.
