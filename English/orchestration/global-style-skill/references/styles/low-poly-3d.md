# Low-poly 3D

Source: supplied reference board 08, label “Low-poly 3D”. Dimension: illustration and rendering technique.

## Observed

The example uses visibly faceted mountains, trees, animals, and water to form a stylized outdoor scene. Large geometric surfaces and a blue-green environment define the asset treatment. The screenshot demonstrates a rendered appearance; it does not establish a live three-dimensional scene, camera interaction, or animation.

## Suggested use and combinations

Consider the technique for an educational explanation, game-related introduction, campaign, or product story when the subject benefits from a simplified spatial world. Select relevant content rather than adding a landscape solely for visual novelty. Pair the scene with plain explanatory typography and a clear action area. A restrained layout can keep the asset dominant without extending faceted forms to controls. Page and interaction owners determine the real content and behavior.

## Limits, assets, and fallback

Important labels, distinctions, and relationships should not depend on tiny objects or subtle shading of polygon faces. Check meaningful silhouettes and crop boundaries at smaller sizes. A coherent pre-rendered asset is often enough; modeling adds preparation, and real-time rendering adds implementation and performance work that needs its own justification. No interactive scene is required by this reference. If suitable assets are unavailable, fall back to a flat vector landscape, a simple explanatory diagram, or a text-led section appropriate to the subject.
